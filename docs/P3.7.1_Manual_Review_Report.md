### Finding 1 — Duplicate resume-version content appears in generated answers

**Observed behaviour.** Four of the seven answered questions emit two near-identical statements that differ only by document version. In MV-02 (lines 20–30, 56–66) the profile-summary chunk is quoted twice: `3d6b22d807ac3dfa` from v2.2 at offsets 262–878, and `9a683663fc00a409` from v2.3 at offsets 262–883. The two texts diverge in exactly one clause — *"…for Fortune 500 data migration programs including Maruti Suzuki…"* against *"…for large-scale enterprise data migration programs for Maruti Suzuki…"*. MV-02 also quotes the heading `Cloud Platforms` twice with **byte-identical text** from the two documents (`fade71eed2d4cb06`, v2.2, 2650–2665; `5bbad21cc17e5b50`, v2.3, 2653–2668). The same duplicated profile summary appears in MV-03 (lines 91–113), MV-05 (lines 233–255) and MV-08 (lines 389–447). Both quotations land in the assembled `answer_text`, so the duplication is visible to a terminal user, not merely present in the structured payload.

**Contract status: CONFORMING.** Every duplicated statement cites its own `document_id` and quotes its own span verbatim. `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §21 places *"multi-document synthesis policy, contradiction resolution between the two committed resume versions"* explicitly out of scope, and §23 Q-5 records the Repository Owner's disposition: the contract *"requires each statement to cite its `document_id`; it does not require the Generator to prefer one version"*, with version preference assigned to **Canonical Document Marking — an explicitly separate, un-started piece of work.** No guarantee is violated. G-7 (support by construction), G-11 (verbatim evidence) and G-12 (traceability) all hold on every duplicated statement.

**Repository impact.** Answer-text readability only. No traceability, determinism, or evidence guarantee is affected; the reviewer can tell the two quotations apart precisely *because* each carries its document identity.

**ALTM attribution — `ALTM-RETRIEVE-4`**, *"Low precision on a known-answerable query"* (`docs/altm.md` §5; identifier derived in `docs/P3.3.4_Retrieval_Diagnosis_Report.md` §2, transcribed in `evaluation/altm_rules.py`). Its documented investigation is verbatim *"Check for near-duplicate or loosely-related chunks crowding results"* — which is exactly the observed mechanism: the two committed resume versions supply near-duplicate chunks, and both survive into the top-5. The rule fires for **10 of 22** benchmark questions (P3.3.4 §3; unchanged in P3.3.5 §5).

**`ALTM-KNOWLEDGE-1` is deliberately not claimed.** *"Answer cites the wrong document version"* remains **not activated**: `docs/P3.3.5_Evaluation_Record_Enrichment_Report.md` §4 determines that document identity establishes *which* document was retrieved but never which version is *current*, since only Manifest filenames encode `v2_2`/`v2_3` and canonical-document designation does not exist. `ALTM-KNOWLEDGE-2` is **excluded on evidence** in the same section. Attributing this finding to the Knowledge stage would therefore be a reinterpretation, which this sprint may not perform.

**Retrieval Diagnosis reference.** `docs/P3.3.4_Retrieval_Diagnosis_Report.md` §5 recurring pattern 2 (*"precision is uniformly capped"* — every question with relevant evidence at exactly 0.2) and §7 observation 7 (*"`ALTM-RETRIEVE-4` fires for every full-recall question"*). `docs/P3.3.5_…` §5 records three benchmark questions retrieving **v2.2 exclusively** where v2.3 was expected — the same version-crowding mechanism, measured.

**Prior sprint report reference.** `docs/P3.5.2_Generation_Implementation_Report.md` §9 limitation 4, in full: *"The two-version corpus produces near-duplicate statements. Where retrieval returns the corresponding sections of both committed resume versions, the answer carries both quotations with distinct `document_id` values. That is correct under this contract… but it is visible in the assembled `answer_text`."*

> **This is confirmation of an already-diagnosed repository behaviour, not a new discovery.** P3.5.2 predicted this exact behaviour, described the exact mechanism, and recorded it as an accepted limitation before the CLI existed. The manual review observed it in the terminal, on the predicted path, with the predicted document-identity annotation intact.

### Finding 2 — Lexical retrieval returns low-relevance evidence for unsupported questions

**Observed behaviour.** MV-08 asked *"Describe your Rust programming experience."* The CLI returned `outcome: "Answer"` with five statements, **none of which mentions Rust** (lines 385–447): the profile summary from both versions, the bare heading `Programming & Automation`, the bare heading `PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE`, and a project title. The corpus contains no Rust content; the match is carried by generic query tokens. MV-04 shows the milder form on a *supported* question — *"Which databases have you worked with?"* returned the `Databases` heading but then three chunks about an RCA agent, a FastAPI/GitHub integration and stakeholder coordination, rather than the chunk enumerating database technologies. The contrasting case is MV-07: *"What Kubernetes operators have you written?"* shares no term with any chunk, retrieves nothing, and abstains.

**Contract status: CONFORMING, and the boundary is precise.** The Generator did not overstate. Every statement is a verbatim quotation of retrieved evidence, so no claim about Rust was made, invented, or implied — the answer is *irrelevant*, not *unfaithful*. `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §23 Q-4 records this in advance: *"Milestone 1A's answers are verbatim quotations. Is that sufficient as an answer, or only as a grounded answer?"* — with impact *"None for Milestone 1A — Answer Relevancy is a Final Answer-stage metric owned by the Evaluation Engine in Milestone 2"*.

**Retrieval-stage attribution.** Per `docs/altm.md` §7 (upstream-first) and §10 Principle 1 and 4, the symptom surfaces at the Final Answer stage but the **first check that fails is Retrieve**: the evidence set the Generator was handed contained nothing relevant, and the Generator faithfully quoted what it was given. `docs/P3.5.2_…` §10 observation 2 makes the corollary structural rather than argued — `ALTM-INFER-2` (*"False confidence on an unanswerable question"*) **cannot occur**, because the abstention text makes no claim about the corpus and every statement is a byte-for-byte quotation. Attribution to Infer would be a misdiagnosis of exactly the kind ALTM §10 Principle 4 warns against.

**ALTM attribution — `ALTM-RETRIEVE-4`**, *"Low precision on a known-answerable query"*, whose investigation clause — *"Check for near-duplicate or loosely-related chunks crowding results"* — names the **loosely-related** half of the mechanism here. The MV-04 form is the same rule on a benchmark-shaped question. For a question the corpus cannot answer at all, `docs/altm.md` §5 offers no row whose premise holds (every retrieval row presumes a *known-answerable* query), so no rule is claimed beyond RETRIEVE-4; this is recorded, not resolved.

**Retrieval Metrics reference.** `docs/P3.3.3_Retrieval_Metrics_Report.md` §4: **Chunk Precision@K macro 0.1273, micro 0.1308**, against Chunk Recall@K macro 0.5227. §4 further records that *"Ten questions score precision 0.2 and recall 1.0"* — retrieval finds the right evidence and surrounds it with four irrelevant chunks. §6 observation 4: precision is bounded above by `expected_count / top_k`, with `top_k` fixed at 5 while 21 of 22 questions expect fewer than 5 chunks. Values unchanged after enrichment (`docs/P3.3.5_…` §3).

**Retrieval Diagnosis reference.** `docs/P3.3.4_Retrieval_Diagnosis_Report.md` §3 — **every one of the 22 diagnoses localizes to Retrieve**; zero to Knowledge, zero to Index. §5 recurring pattern 2: *"No question exceeds 0.2."* Post-enrichment attribution (`docs/P3.3.5_…` §5): `ALTM-RETRIEVE-1` ×3, `ALTM-RETRIEVE-2` ×5, `ALTM-RETRIEVE-3` ×4, `ALTM-RETRIEVE-4` ×10, all 22 at Complete Evidence.

**Prior sprint report reference.** `docs/P3.6.0_CLI_Integration_Report.md` §9 limitation 6 records that the abstention path *"is reachable only by lexical disjointness"* — the exact discriminator MV-07 (disjoint → Abstain) and MV-08 (partially overlapping → Answer) exhibit from the terminal.

G-8's biconditional — `outcome == Abstain` exactly when `statements` is empty — holds in both cases, and P3.5.2 §8 D1 records that it is **structural rather than checked**: one emptiness test decides both fields, so they cannot disagree. The outcome domain is the two Milestone 1A values; `Clarify` is defined by `docs/roadmap.md` §2.4 and correctly never emitted (§9.1).

**What the manual review added.** `docs/P3.5.2_…` §9 limitation 6 records that *"the Abstain path is unreachable over the committed corpus at default `top_k`"* — all 22 benchmark questions retrieve at least one chunk, so the entire abstention path was exercised **synthetically** in the specification suite. MV-07 is the first execution of that path over the **real committed corpus, from the terminal, on a real question.** It behaved exactly as the synthetic specifications required. `docs/P3.6.0_…` §9 limitation 4 predicted MV-09's behaviour precisely: an empty `--question` is well-formed, *"tokenizes to nothing, retrieves nothing, and abstains through the pipeline's own contracted path."*
