# WORK PACKAGE 2 — Repository Constitutional Principles

Documented only where repository evolution demonstrates them. Nothing below is aspirational.

The two categories are kept separate because they bind differently. **Constitutional Principles** govern how the repository is *governed* — they survive milestone completion, tool change and architectural revision. **Engineering Doctrine** governs how the repository *builds* — it is falsifiable by engineering evidence and may be revised when evidence contradicts it. Conflating them would make an engineering preference as hard to change as a governance guarantee.

## A. Constitutional Principles

Enduring repository governance. Each is demonstrated, not asserted.

### CP-1 — Governance precedes implementation

Architectural and scope decisions are written and frozen before implementation begins.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/roadmap.md` §6 (*"Docs before code"*) and `docs/architecture.md` §2, both frozen at Milestone 0.5, before any pipeline code existed. The pattern held under pressure at every layer: `docs/DOCUMENT_CONTRACT.md` frozen at P2.5 before Construction at P3.1.x; `docs/CHUNK_CONTRACT.md` frozen at P2.1 before the Chunker; `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` frozen at P3.5.1 before `sample_rag/generator.py` at P3.5.2; `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` build item 1's Knowledge Manifest schema frozen at P1.2.0 before `scripts/build_manifest.py` at P1.2.1.

### CP-2 — Repository Owner decisions resolve governance where repository evidence alone is insufficient

Repository governance precedes implementation. Where evidence underdetermines a disposition, the Repository Owner decides — and an implementing agent may recommend but never decide.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/ENGINEERING_TRACEABILITY_REGISTER.md` §2 standing rule, established at P3.1.7.1: *"architectural disposition is not the implementing agent's decision. An implementing agent presents options and a recommendation; a recommendation is not a decision."* Applied three times before this document: `ADR-P3.1.7.2-F2` (two independent reviews reached opposite verdicts on F-2; P3.1.7.1 declined to resolve and escalated); `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §22 G-1 and G-2 (Repository Owner, Sprint P3.5.1-G); `docs/ENGINEERING_TRACEABILITY_REGISTER.md` §3.7 (owner-approved governance deviation waiving §13's synthetic-negative criterion for DQ-4 alone).

### CP-3 — Historical reports are immutable

A committed report is an accurate statement of repository state at the commit that produced it. It is never edited, even when later found inaccurate. Corrections are recorded forward.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/P3.7.2_…` §2 rule 1 and §4.3. Two inaccuracies were found inside historical reports; both were recorded in the register; **neither was corrected in place.** Also `docs/P3.7.2_…` §4.4: report coverage for Sprints P3.2.0–P3.2.4 and P3.3.1 is missing, and the sprint declined to reconstruct it — *"reconstructing a report for a past sprint would manufacture history."* Immutability binds in both directions: no edit, and no retroactive creation.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/P3.1.7.1_Decision_Gate_Report_Evidence_Verification.md` independently reproduced every finding from the two P3.1.7 reviews **before** Sprint P3.1.7.2 acted on any of them — and the verification changed outcomes: NEW-1 was **REJECTED** as unsupported, NEW-2 **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED** with its survivor count corrected. `docs/ENGINEERING_TRACEABILITY_REGISTER.md` §4 preserves the lesson: *"one review measured specification adequacy; the other asserted it."*

Completion produces a frozen, digest-verified, inspectable artifact — not a partial state.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` **Architecture Freeze**; `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` closing line (*"a frozen repository authority"*); `docs/DOCUMENT_CONTRACT.md` §8.2–§8.8 verified byte-for-byte unchanged across Erratum E-1. Deferring a capability records *when* it is built. It never records that it will not be built.

**Demonstrated by:** `docs/roadmap.md` §7 (*"Deferred intentionally, not forgotten"*); `docs/architecture.md` §11 (same construction); `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` Out of Scope (*"All of the above are Milestone 2+ concerns, already deferred"*). ## Decision 1 — Repository Engineering Philosophy

> ### The repository builds the instrument that measures the system before it builds the system, and it proves the instrument deterministically before allowing anything probabilistic near it.

Four commitments constitute this philosophy. Each is derived from repository evidence, not from intent.

**1. Ground truth precedes measurement.** `docs/roadmap.md` §2: *"A passing DeepEval assertion says nothing about system quality if the underlying 'correct answer' was never verified."* The Golden Dataset, QA pairs and Evidence Trace Dataset were built and validated by 71 specifications at P3.4.1 before any metric was reported against them.

**2. Determinism is the precondition for trust, not a phase to pass through.** The North Star Question — *"Can we deterministically trace every answer back to verified knowledge, without relying on any probabilistic AI component?"* — is gated: *"If no — do not proceed to Milestone 2."* It is now answered **yes, by demonstration** (`docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` North Star section; `docs/P3.7.1_…` Finding 3): all 45 statements emitted across the manual review carry a chunk id, a document id and document-frame offsets, and three separate processes produced indistinguishable output.

**3. Every claim is traceable to a committed artifact.** 372 of 372 specifications are mapped to a claim family and an owning sprint (`docs/ENGINEERING_TRACEABILITY_REGISTER.md` §7, §7.1). Five repository authorities are digest-verified across four sprints. Sixteen governance modifications at P3.7.2 each carry a trace; **zero were made without one.**

**4. What the repository does not know, it records rather than resolves.** `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §23 retains Q-3, Q-4 and Q-5 as *"intentionally unresolved… retained to preserve architectural history."* `docs/ENGINEERING_TRACEABILITY_REGISTER.md` §3.5 carries five observations that are recorded *"because they have a disposition, not because they are scheduled."* `docs/P3.3.5_…` §4 declines to activate `ALTM-KNOWLEDGE-1` because it is not determinable.

**The philosophy in one sentence:** *this repository treats an unverified claim as more dangerous than an unbuilt capability* — which is why it can close a milestone with three Definition of Done items unmet and say so plainly, and why it will not close one on an assertion it has not measured.

## Decision 2 — Milestone Constitutional Boundaries

Milestone boundaries are drawn where the **kind of engineering changes**, per **CP-6**. Each boundary is formally defined below with its engineering rationale.

| Milestone | Engineering Objective | Boundary Condition (what changes at entry) |
|---|---|---|
| **Milestone 1A** | **Deterministic Knowledge Pipeline** | Nothing probabilistic exists. Every stage is stdlib, reproducible, and byte-identical across runs |
| **Milestone 1B** | **Retrieval Infrastructure Foundation** | The remaining **deterministic** infrastructure is completed — Index Layer, corpus expansion, chunk/index validation. Still nothing probabilistic |
| **Milestone 2** | **AI-Enabled Retrieval & Generation** | The first probabilistic component enters the repository. Every one replaces an implementation behind a contract that already exists |
| **Milestone 3** | **Production Evaluation & Regression** | Evaluation becomes comparative across versions rather than absolute within one |

### 2.1 Milestone 1A — Deterministic Knowledge Pipeline

**Objective:** Establish that every answer can be traced deterministically to verified knowledge, with no probabilistic component anywhere in the pipeline.

**Rationale.** This is `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s own North Star Question, and it is a *gating* objective: the document states that a "no" forbids proceeding to Milestone 2. The boundary is therefore not a quantity of work but a proof obligation, and the proof obligation is discharged — `docs/P3.7.1_…` Finding 3 and §6.4; `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` North Star section.

**Constitutional status:** the objective is **met**. Three Definition of Done items are **not met**. `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s own record states both plainly: *"The North Star Question and the Definition of Done are answered separately, and only the first is answered yes."* Decision 5 addresses the second without touching the first.

### 2.2 Milestone 1B — Retrieval Infrastructure Foundation

**Objective:** Complete the deterministic retrieval infrastructure — the Index Layer, its interfaces, the corpus it operates over, and the validation that covers both — so that Milestone 2 can replace implementations behind contracts that already exist.

**This is the constitutional establishment of Milestone 1B.** It is a **Repository Owner ruling.** No committed authority establishes this milestone; `docs/roadmap.md` §1 currently denies it. The reasoning is therefore given in full.

**Rationale 1 — Milestone 2's own constitutional precondition is currently false.**
`docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s Governing Principle binds Milestone 2, not only Milestone 1A:

> *"Every Milestone 2 component must replace an implementation behind an existing contract rather than introduce a new contract. Milestone 2 extends implementations; it should not reshape interfaces established here."*

`EmbeddingProvider` and `VectorStore` **do not exist** in the repository — re-verified at `8b2d387`, one grep hit, in a docstring recording the blocker. If Milestone 2 began today, its first act would be to *introduce* the `EmbeddingProvider` contract, in direct violation of the principle governing it. Milestone 1B exists to make Milestone 2's own precondition true before Milestone 2 begins. **No other allocation of these capabilities achieves that.**

**Rationale 2 — Folding them into Milestone 2 recreates the exact failure mode interface-first design exists to prevent.**
`docs/architecture.md` §7 states the reason implementations are deferred:

> *"Proving plumbing correctness with deterministic stubs first means Milestone 2's real integrations are validated against an already-correct pipeline, rather than debugging pipeline logic and model behavior simultaneously."*

If the `Indexer` and BGE embeddings land in the same milestone, the first sprint to introduce a real embedding model is also the first sprint to introduce an indexing stage. A retrieval defect at that point is not attributable: it may be the model, the index, or the plumbing. `docs/altm.md` §10 principle 1 — *"one failure has one primary origin"* — becomes undiagnosable by construction. The repository has already paid to avoid exactly this and would be discarding what it paid for.

**Rationale 3 — The four blocking capabilities are homogeneous, and their homogeneity is deterministic.**
Index Layer, placeholder vectors, job-description corpus, JobOps SQL filtering, DQ-5, DQ-6, DQ-7: **not one of them requires a model, a network call, or a non-deterministic value.** Every one is stdlib-implementable under `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s own library table. They share a boundary condition with Milestone 1A, not with Milestone 2.

**Rationale 4 — The roadmap's denial does not cover this case, and the ruling is consistent with its intent.**
`docs/roadmap.md` §1 denies a milestone for *"retrieval evaluation and generation evaluation"*, on the stated ground that both are *"gated behind a working deterministic pipeline from Milestone 1A."* Milestone 1B as established here contains **no retrieval evaluation and no generation evaluation** — Ragas and DeepEval remain in Milestone 2, exactly as the roadmap directs. Milestone 1B contains only the deterministic infrastructure that the gate itself is made of. The roadmap's reasoning is preserved; only its literal text requires amendment, and that amendment is authorized in Work Package 4 rather than performed here.

**Rationale 5 — Why "1B" rather than "1.5" or a renumbered Milestone 2.**
The repository already uses a suffixed milestone for a phase that extends rather than advances the state of the system: Milestone 0.5 was documentation-only. "1B" keeps the deterministic phase numbered under 1, signalling that it is a continuation of the Milestone 1A engineering kind. Renumbering Milestone 2 or 3 would invalidate milestone references across `docs/architecture.md` §9, `docs/altm.md` §12, `docs/roadmap.md` §1, `docs/glossary.md`, `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §23 Q-4 and every P3.x report — violating **CP-3** by making committed history read falsely.

**Boundary condition (binding):** Milestone 1B introduces **no** embedding model, vector database, BM25 implementation, LLM, or evaluation tool. Milestone 1A's criterion A-5 — *"zero imports of any embedding, vector-store, or LLM-evaluation library"* — **remains binding throughout Milestone 1B**, and the AST allowlist specifications that enforce it remain in force unchanged.

### 2.3 Milestone 2 — AI-Enabled Retrieval & Generation

**Objective:** Replace deterministic implementations with real probabilistic ones behind the interfaces frozen in Milestones 1A and 1B, and activate the two evaluation layers that measure them.

**Rationale.** This boundary is where non-determinism enters the repository, and it is the sharpest boundary in the system because it is the point at which the repository's central guarantee changes character: before it, correctness is *proved* by byte-identity; after it, correctness is *measured* by metrics against ground truth. `docs/roadmap.md` §5 assigns Layer 2 (Ragas → Retrieve) and Layer 3 (DeepEval → Infer) precisely here, and `docs/altm.md` §12 records the reason: *"Retrieve and Infer stages become measurable for real."* The Golden Dataset, Evidence Trace Dataset and 372 specifications exist so that this transition is measurable on the day it happens.

**Constitutional constraint:** every Milestone 2 component **replaces an implementation behind an existing contract**. Milestone 2 introduces no new interface. This is `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s Governing Principle, and Milestone 1B exists to make it satisfiable.

### 2.4 Milestone 3 — Production Evaluation & Regression

**Objective:** Make quality comparable across repository versions, and harden the evaluation lab for repeated production-grade use.

**Rationale.** Layers 1–3 answer *"is this version correct?"* Layer 4 answers a different question — *"did this change silently degrade previously-correct behaviour?"* — and `docs/roadmap.md` §5 records why it cannot be folded into the others: Promptfoo is *"not a pipeline stage at runtime. A comparison methodology applied across two full pipeline runs."* A regression harness requires two comparable baselines to exist; before Milestone 2 completes, there is only one, and it is deterministic, so a diff over it is trivially empty. `docs/altm.md` §12 states the same boundary: *"Evaluate stage becomes measurable across versions."*

### 2.5 Boundary integrity

| **One kind of engineering per milestone** | 1A/1B deterministic; 2 probabilistic; 3 comparative |
| **No milestone reshapes an earlier milestone's interfaces** | `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` Governing Principle; enforced by 1B completing the interface set before 2 begins |
| **Every milestone ends inspectable** | **CP-5**; `docs/roadmap.md` §6 *"Small, demonstrable milestones"* |

## Decision 4 — Constitutional Reasoning

Every allocation above, with its four required components. Grouped only where capabilities share an identical rationale, and grouped entries name every capability they cover.

### 4.1 Milestone 1B reasoning

#### R-1B-01/02 — `EmbeddingProvider` and `VectorStore` interfaces

**Repository evidence.** `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` criterion A-1 requires both *"defined and swappable."* `docs/architecture.md` §5 scopes them *"1A — interface + stub only"* and *"1A — interface only, no implementation."* §7 gives their Protocol shapes. `grep -rn "EmbeddingProvider\|VectorStore" --include=*.py` at `8b2d387` returns **one hit**, a docstring in `tests/test_data_quality.py` recording the blocker. `docs/P3.7.2_…` §5.1 classifies the absence as **Blocks Milestone 1A**.

**Repository Owner reasoning.** This is the single most consequential absence in the repository, because it is the one capability whose absence makes a *later* milestone's governing constraint unsatisfiable. Every other gap costs coverage; this one costs Milestone 2 its constitutional footing. Its absence was recorded four separate times — DQV §16 O-6, the register §5, the Build Item Matrix, and P3.7.2 §5.1 — without an owning sprint ever being assigned. Four recordings and no owner is precisely the condition **CP-2** exists to resolve.

**Why 1B.** The interface must exist *before* the implementation that fills it, or Milestone 2 introduces a contract instead of replacing an implementation. Placing it in 1B is the only allocation under which `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s Governing Principle remains true on the day Milestone 2 opens.

**Why not 1A.** Milestone 1A is closing on a manually reviewed, digest-verified baseline (`docs/P3.7.1_…`, 24 PASS / 0 FAIL against `b50e45f`). Adding an unbuilt component now reopens the baseline the review accepted, invalidating the acceptance without producing better evidence — and, per **CP-5**, a milestone that reopens its frozen baseline to add work has not ended.

#### R-1B-03/04 — Index Layer (`Indexer`) and deterministic placeholder vectors

**Repository evidence.** `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` build item 3. `docs/architecture.md` §5 (`Indexer.index(chunks) -> Index`), §9, §4 (Index is one of the six stages). Build Item Matrix, item 3: *"Structure-aware chunking with recursive-character fallback shipped… **No `Indexer`, no `EmbeddingProvider`, no placeholder vectors exist in the repository**."* `docs/altm.md` §12 nonetheless records the Index stage as *"fully exercised"* at Milestone 1A via Layer 1.

**Repository Owner reasoning.** The repository currently has an Index *stage* in its architecture, an Index *stage* in its diagnostic model, and no Index *component*. Chunking discharges part of the stage's responsibility, which is why the gap survived undetected for so long. The placeholder-vector requirement is not decoration: build item 4's note establishes that placeholder values must be *"meaningful, not arbitrary… so Milestone 2 swaps values inside an already-correct shape rather than changing the shape itself."* A stub whose values are unspecified provides no such shape.

**Why 1B.** These are deterministic, stdlib, reproducible components under Milestone 1A's own library table. They belong with the deterministic infrastructure, and they must precede M2-01.

**Why not 1A.** Same baseline argument as R-1B-01/02, plus: build item 3 has **no owning sprint** in the entire repository history. Assigning an unowned build item to a milestone that is closing would be assigning it to no one.

#### R-1B-05/06/07/12 — Job Description corpus, JobOps ingest, SQL filtering, `job_*`/`jobops_*` dataset population

**Repository evidence.** `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` F-1 (*"at least one job description"*) and F-2 (*"real JobOps data, including at least one exclusion-criteria case"*), both unchecked with evidence at the Acceptance Criteria status table. `sample_rag/knowledge_manifest.json` catalogues two resume documents. `sample_rag/retriever.py` module docstring: the SQL stage *"is not exercised: it filters JobOps-derived structured metadata… and the corpus contains no such data. Not exercising it reflects the corpus, and changes no architecture."* `diagnostics["sql_filter_applied"]` is `False`; unapplied filters are reported in `filters_ignored` rather than dropped. **Decisively**, `datasets/SCHEMA.md` §9 already deferred exactly this: Job Descriptions and JobOps *"remain schema-valid empty stubs… until scraped job posting data is stable enough to extract from"* / *"until the underlying SQLite schema fields are settled"*, with the closing sentence *"Populating job and JobOps facts is explicitly out of scope for Sprint 1A.1 and belongs to a subsequent sprint once its source-data preconditions are met."*

**Repository Owner reasoning.** This ruling **does not defer anything new.** `datasets/SCHEMA.md` deferred corpus population on stated engineering grounds long before Milestone 1A implementation began, and gave the reason: *"the representation interface is finalized for all three sources now, so that job and JobOps population in a later sprint requires no schema rework — only content."* The acceptance criteria were written as though that deferral would resolve inside Milestone 1A; it did not, because its precondition is **external** — JobOps' SQLite schema must settle, and `docs/architecture.md` §3 makes JobOps read-only and outside this repository's control. This decision names the *"subsequent sprint"* that `datasets/SCHEMA.md` already anticipated. It also confirms that F-2 needs **data, not code**: the filter stage is implemented and reports its own non-application honestly.

**Why 1B.** Corpus expansion is deterministic work that changes what the corpus *is*, not how it is processed probabilistically. It is also the precondition for 1B-10 (DQ-7 needs chunks from more than one document class), 1B-13 (O-5's vacuity clears only at corpus scale > 1) and 1B-15 (JobOps-as-`Document` is *"structurally excluded today by the manifest discovery gate"*).

**Why not 1A.** The precondition is external to this repository and no authority establishes it as met. Blocking a milestone on an unmet external precondition converts a deterministic engineering objective into an indefinite one — and Milestone 1A's objective (the North Star Question) is already demonstrably met without it, over a corpus that produced 172 chunks, 22 benchmark questions and 372 specifications.

#### R-1B-08/09 — DQ-5 and DQ-6

## Decision 5 — Milestone 1A Boundary Amendment

### 5.1 The constitutional ruling

> **1.** Milestone 1A establishes the repository's **deterministic engineering baseline**. Its objective — the North Star Question — is met and demonstrated.
>
> **2.** **Retrieval Infrastructure capabilities are constitutionally reassigned to Milestone 1B**: the `EmbeddingProvider` and `VectorStore` interfaces, the Index Layer and its deterministic placeholder vectors, the Job Description and JobOps corpus expansion, exercised SQL filtering, and DQ-5, DQ-6 and DQ-7 (Decision 3 §3.1, capabilities 1B-01 … 1B-16).
>
> **3.** **AI capabilities remain assigned to Milestone 2**, unchanged (Decision 3 §3.2).
>
> **4.** **Production regression capabilities remain assigned to Milestone 3**, unchanged (Decision 3 §3.3).
>
> **5.** The Milestone 1A **Definition of Done, Acceptance Criteria, In Scope, Out of Scope and Success Criteria remain textually unchanged.** The reassignment is recorded as an **appended Repository Owner Scope Ruling**, citing this decision as authority.

### 5.2 Why this method, and not the alternatives

Three constructions were available. `docs/P3.7.2_…` §6.7 named the first two and correctly declined to choose between them.

| Construction | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **(a) Implement the four blocking items inside Milestone 1A before closure** | **Rejected.** It reopens a baseline that has already been manually reviewed (`docs/P3.7.1_…`, 24 PASS / 0 FAIL against `b50e45f`) and digest-verified across four sprints. Every artifact accepted by that review would need re-acceptance, and the review's own value — that it observed the *committed* system, not a system in motion — would be spent. It also blocks closure on **1B-05/1B-06**, whose precondition (`datasets/SCHEMA.md` §9) is external to this repository and not established as met. Milestone 1A would close on an external dependency it does not control |
| **(b) Reword the Definition of Done to narrow it** | **Rejected, and barred.** **CP-9** forbids it, and P3.7.2 refused the same action for a smaller change — declining even to amend build item 6's diagram, because *"amending the diagram would redefine milestone scope."* A narrowed Definition of Done would also destroy the record: a future reader could not tell that F-1, F-2 and A-1 were ever required, nor that they were met by reassignment rather than by never having been asked |
| **(c) Preserve the Definition of Done verbatim; append a Repository Owner Scope Ruling recording the reassignment and its authority** | **Adopted.** The original requirement stays legible, the reassignment is explicit and attributable, and the milestone closes on what it actually demonstrated |

Construction (c) is not novel in this repository — it is the third application of an established method:

- `docs/DOCUMENT_CONTRACT.md` §8.9 — Erratum E-1 stated a missing invariant in a **new section** while §8.2–§8.8 stayed byte-for-byte frozen.
- `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` build item 4 — amended the `Retriever` interface by **recording the amendment**, not by editing `docs/architecture.md` §5. `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` §22 names this as the precedent the `Generator` amendment should follow.
- **This ruling** — reassigns scope by appending, leaving every scope statement intact.

### 5.3 This preserves architectural layering; it does not reduce repository scope

| Property | Before this ruling | After this ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Capabilities required by the repository | Index Layer, `EmbeddingProvider`, `VectorStore`, JD corpus, JobOps SQL, DQ-5/6/7 | **Identical set. None removed** |
| Capabilities with an owning milestone | 0 of those 8 (three had **no owning sprint**) | **8 of 8** |
| Milestone 1A Definition of Done text | 7 items | **7 items, unchanged, verbatim** |
| Milestone 1A acceptance criteria text | 9 criteria, 3 unchecked with evidence | **9 criteria, unchanged; the 3 remain unchecked with evidence** |
| Milestone 2's governing precondition (*replace behind an existing contract*) | **Unsatisfiable** — `EmbeddingProvider` does not exist | **Satisfiable** — Milestone 1B establishes it before Milestone 2 opens |
| Failure attribution at the Index/Retrieve boundary in Milestone 2 | Ambiguous — model, index and plumbing would land together | **Attributable** — plumbing proven deterministically first |

**The repository's total scope is unchanged by this decision.** What changes is *when* eight capabilities are built and *who owns them*. That is engineering sequencing, and **CP-7** governs it: *"Deferring a capability records when it is built. It never records that it will not be built."*

One consequence is stated rather than implied, per **CP-8**: after this ruling, `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s Definition of Done items 1 and 2 remain **textually unmet**, and the Scope Ruling records *why* — the capabilities they name now belong to Milestone 1B. Milestone 1A therefore closes with its Definition of Done annotated rather than satisfied, and the annotation says so in those words. A closure that claimed satisfaction would be the first false statement in this repository's governance record.

# WORK PACKAGE 5 — Repository Legacy

The permanent engineering legacy established by Milestone 1A. Every entry is a property the repository now *has*, evidenced by a committed artifact — not a claim about effort.

### Deterministic retrieval

`sample_rag/retriever.py` returns a `RetrievalResult` on every path, with every field populated and none ever `None`, over a corpus of **172 chunks**. Retrieval is a fixed function of query and corpus: no timestamp, no random value, no learned parameter. The contract froze `-> RetrievalResult` *"not a bare `List[Chunk]`"* before implementation, and 117 evaluation, 48 generation and 27 CLI specifications now depend on that shape without depending on how a score is computed.

### Deterministic generation

`sample_rag/generator.py` implements `docs/GENERATION_CONTRACT.md` v1.0.0 — eleven fields, thirteen invariants, fourteen guarantees — under **48 specifications mapping every guarantee**, with **25 of 25 mutants killed**. Determinism is specified by exclusion (G-9): no timestamp, duration, random, hash-seeded, locale-dependent or floating-point value is constructed anywhere in the module. Three separate processes, given identical input, produced indistinguishable output at the terminal.

### Evidence traceability

Every one of the **45 statements** emitted across the manual review carries a chunk id, a document id and document-frame character offsets, and each statement's text is byte-identical to its supporting span. An answer is auditable from the artifact alone, with no access to the running system. Abstention is as traceable as an answer: `diagnostics` remains fully populated when `statements` is empty.

### Executable specifications

**372 specifications** across eleven families, mapped 372-of-372 to a claim family and an owning sprint. Two families are enforced **structurally rather than behaviourally** — AST-parsed allowlists constraining what a module may contain and import — because *"a behavioural test cannot observe a branch being added; these can."*

### Governance discipline

Sixteen governance modifications at Sprint P3.7.2, **every one traced to a committed authority; zero made without one.** Two inaccuracies found inside historical reports, both recorded, **neither corrected in place**. Three acceptance criteria left unchecked with the evidence establishing they are unmet, rather than quietly marked. Five repository authority digests identical across four sprints.

### Mutation-tested validation

Five mutation rounds — 18, 23, 34, 25, 25 mutants — with every genuine survivor classified and every equivalent mutant justified. **Twice, the mutation round found a defect in a specification rather than in an implementation**, and both were fixed and re-verified before their sprints closed. The standing caution travels with every figure: *"a surviving mutant is evidence of a blind spot; a killed mutant is not proof of adequacy."*

### Manual acceptance

**16 manual verifications** across six execution paths, recorded as a **705-line verbatim terminal transcript** — shell prompts, queued-command echoes and all — then assessed independently against **24 verification items, 24 PASS / 0 FAIL**. The assessment distinguished what was *observed* from what a committed specification *guarantees*, on four items, *"because an acceptance record that cannot distinguish 'observed' from 'specified' is not an acceptance record."* It created **zero backlog items**, because both behavioural findings had been predicted in writing by earlier sprints.

A durable register spanning six layers, indexing findings, ADRs, cross-sprint observations, review dispositions and claim-to-specification mappings — established to end a specific defect: an engineering record held in `conftest.py` docstrings, module docstrings and commit messages. # WORK PACKAGE 6 — Repository Transition Strategy

```text
Milestone 1A
Deterministic Knowledge Pipeline
        │
        ▼
Milestone 1B
Retrieval Infrastructure Foundation
        │
        ▼
Milestone 2
AI-Enabled Retrieval & Generation
        │
        ▼
Milestone 3
Production Evaluation & Regression
```

### Transition 1A → 1B — *from proven plumbing to complete deterministic infrastructure*

**What changes:** the Index Layer is built; `EmbeddingProvider` and `VectorStore` are defined; the corpus grows beyond two resume versions to include job descriptions and JobOps structured data; DQ-5, DQ-6 and DQ-7 close the Data Quality Validation set; `documents[].indexed` acquires a determinable meaning.

**What does not change:** determinism. Criterion **A-5 remains binding** and its AST allowlist specifications remain in force. No model, vector database, BM25 implementation, LLM or evaluation tool enters the repository at this transition.

**Engineering rationale.** Milestone 1A proved that a deterministic pipeline can trace every answer to verified knowledge. It proved this over a corpus of one document class through a pipeline missing one stage. Milestone 1B completes the stage and widens the corpus **while the guarantee still holds**, so that when the guarantee is deliberately relaxed at Milestone 2, exactly one thing has changed. `docs/roadmap.md` §3's Locked Implementation Order — *Chunking → Indexing → Retrieval* — is **restored** by this transition, not departed from.

**Exit condition:** every Milestone 1B capability from Decision 3 §3.1, with `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md` criteria F-1, F-2 and A-1 satisfiable in substance, and the repository still passing a byte-identity determinism specification.

### Transition 1B → 2 — *from deterministic to probabilistic*

**What changes:** the sharpest boundary in the repository. Real embeddings replace placeholder vectors; a real vector store replaces the interface; BM25 and RRF fusion replace distinct-term overlap; DeepSeek replaces the quotation generator; Ragas and DeepEval activate as Layers 2 and 3.

**What does not change:** **every interface.** `docs/MILESTONE_1A.md`'s Governing Principle binds this transition — *"Every Milestone 2 component must replace an implementation behind an existing contract rather than introduce a new contract."* Milestone 1B exists so that this sentence is true when the transition is made.

**Engineering rationale.** This is the transition where the repository's central guarantee changes character: before it, correctness is *proved* by byte-identity; after it, correctness is *measured* by metrics against ground truth. The Golden Dataset (26 facts, 22 QA pairs), the Evidence Trace Dataset (22 entries) and 372 specifications exist so that the change is measured on the day it happens rather than discovered later. `docs/altm.md` §12: *"Retrieve and Infer stages become measurable for real."*

**Exit condition:** every Milestone 2 capability from Decision 3 §3.2, with Layers 2 and 3 reporting against the committed Golden and Evidence Trace datasets — and with the deterministic baseline still runnable for comparison, because Milestone 3 requires it.

### Transition 2 → 3 — *from absolute to comparative*

**What changes:** Promptfoo activates as Layer 4; `reports/baseline/` and `reports/regressions/` acquire content; `REACHABLE_STAGES` widens to the full diagnosable surface; production-readiness hardening lands.

**What does not change:** the pipeline. `docs/roadmap.md` §5 — Layer 4 is *"not a pipeline stage at runtime. A comparison methodology applied across two full pipeline runs."*

**Engineering rationale.** Layers 1–3 answer *"is this version correct?"* Layer 4 answers *"did this change silently degrade previously-correct behaviour?"* — a question that cannot be asked until two comparable versions exist. Attempting it earlier produces a harness that passes while measuring nothing, because a diff over a single byte-identical baseline is trivially empty. `docs/altm.md` §12: *"Evaluate stage becomes measurable across versions."*

**Exit condition:** every Milestone 3 capability from Decision 3 §3.3, with a recorded regression comparison between at least two committed repository versions.

### The invariant across all three transitions

**Every transition adds capability without invalidating the evidence produced before it.** The Golden Dataset validated at Milestone 1A is the same dataset Ragas scores at Milestone 2 and Promptfoo diffs at Milestone 3. The 372 specifications remain runnable. The five authority digests remain verifiable. `docs/altm.md` §12 states the general form: *"Future milestones extend which stages are measurable, not which stages exist."*
